Star chart

The pad is a map of space. Every sequenced step is a star, and the sequence draws a constellation through them in playing order. Modulators are small bodies in orbit around whatever they move. At night it's a ship's console; by day it's a printed star atlas. Switch your device between light and dark to see both.

Stars are steps. A step's star sits where its sound lives on the map, coloured like a real star of that temperature: low, dark sounds glow red, bright ones burn blue-white.

The constellation is the sequence. Lines join the stars in playing order and the playhead travels along them.

Moons show modulation, read-only. A moon circles each star a modulator is moving. You attach and edit them on the modulation page, not here.

Stepped or smooth. The third transport key, matching Slicefield. Stepped captures one position per 16th. Smooth records your gesture as it moves, so the sound glides through a note or ratchet. It only changes how new touches are played and recorded; steps already in the sequence keep whatever they were recorded with. Tap it here.

Ratchet bar. The sound snaps back to 1/16 the instant you let go; the thumb follows a moment later with a small overshoot. Stops are labelled with a tick under each value.

Dice. A longer track, and the die is just a key-sized face with five pips. It rolls whichever page is showing.

Slots. A short list of modulators, each with a live thumbnail of its orbit or envelope, where it's going and how deep. The selected slot is outlined in yellow.

The sky is the target picker. The same chart as the main pad, smaller. Tap a step's star or a drum's fixed star; the selected modulator's moon jumps into orbit around it.

Moves. What it changes: the star's X or Y position on the map, or one engine control (pitch, decay, fold and so on). Colour follows the engine layer it belongs to.

Four knobs, always the same places. Rate (synced to the bar), depth, one shape control (eccentricity for Kepler, detune for Halo, and so on), and phase.

04 · One-shot modulators

Envelopes as events

One-shots fire with each hit and then die away, so they read as events rather than orbits. Each has its own look on the pad and its own envelope shape. The trace shows the envelope with a cursor riding it.

08 · Recording

Gestures, not just moments

A touch or a ratchet hold has a start and an end, and each one gets the catch window. Drag along the timeline to make a gesture: press where it starts, let go where it ends. The steps it records into light up.

50%
  1. Start. A gesture that starts in the first half of a step includes that step. One that starts in the second half begins at the next step: it arrived early for it.
  2. End. Letting go in the first half of a step leaves that step out: you let go a little late. Letting go in the second half keeps it, so holding a ratchet to the end of a step records the whole step.
  3. Taps. A tap that starts and ends inside one step always counts for the step it started in.
  4. Values. While held, the ratchet bar's value for each step is whatever it reads at that step's catch point, so a step always gets a single value for its whole length.
