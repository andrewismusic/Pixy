Star chart

The pad is a map of space. Every sequenced step is a star, and the sequence draws a constellation through them in playing order. Modulators are small moons in orbit around whatever they move.

Stars are steps. A step's star sits where its sound lives on the map, coloured by the sound itself. Brighter and bigger means louder.

The constellation is the sequence. Lines join the stars in playing order. The playhead runs along them in cyan, so you can watch the beat travel across the sky.

Moons are modulators. A moon circles the star it moves. Orbit size is depth, speed is rate, colour is the layer it targets. Steps 5 and 13 carry moons here.

Drums are fixed stars. The 18 drum anchors show as faint points of the background sky. One lights up when a modulator is attached to that whole drum rather than to a step.

Ratchet bar, right of the pad. Rests at 1/16 and springs back when let go. Hold it higher while recording to write ratchets. Drag it here to feel the snap.

Dice in the top bar. One small die, always in reach, randomizes whichever page you're on. Drag it right for stronger rolls.

Swing reads 50 to 75%. 50% is straight, 75% puts the off-beat three quarters of the way to the next 16th. In theory 100% lands on it.

Stellar follows real star classes: deep, low sounds glow like cool red M stars, and bright, high sounds burn blue-white like B stars. It's the most on-theme, and its blues are periwinkle rather than teal, so they stay clear of the playhead. Nebula is what the build uses today. Ion pushes saturation for punchier step tiles.

05 · Modulation

Orbital LFOs

Each LFO is a body in orbit. The trace under each orbit is what the modulator outputs: the body's left-right position over time. These are real orbital solutions, computed live, not drawn curves.

06 · Recording

When does a ratchet move count?

Ratchet bar moves are written per 16th: whatever the bar says applies to the whole step. A move made late in a step is more likely meant for the next one, so each step has a catch window. Drag across the timeline to drop a move and see where it lands.

50%
Tap or drag on the timeline.
  1. Proposal: a move in the first half of a step counts for that step; a move in the second half is held for the next one. That's the same as rounding to the nearest 16th, which is how most drum machines quantize live input.
  2. Letting go of the bar is a move too: it writes 1/16 into the next step it catches, so a ratchet ends cleanly.
  3. While stopped, the bar just changes how fast the pad retriggers under your finger, as now.
