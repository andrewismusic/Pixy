How it works

  • Tap a step to switch it on or off. A new step takes the sound your cursor was last on; switching one off clears it completely, so tapping it back on starts fresh.
  • Hold the pad, then tap a step to give that step the sound under your finger.
  • Hold one or more steps, then touch the pad and those steps take the point you touched last.
  • Only one pointer? Select (the dotted square) latches steps so the pad updates them without needing to hold them down. On a keyboard, hold 1 to 8 and Q to I for steps 1 to 16 while using the mouse on the pad, or focus the pad and use the arrow keys.
  • Hold a step, then tap another to copy the first step's sound onto the second.
  • Knobs: drag up or right to turn up, down or left to turn down. Hold Shift for fine moves, double-tap to reset.
  • Step lanes (the bars icon) add per-step odds: how often a step plays, how often (and how far) it strays from its saved X, Y or decay when it does.
  • The ratchet bar beside the pad rests at 1/16 and springs back when you let go. Hold it higher to cut each step into 2, 3, 4 or 6 hits. While recording, whatever it reads is written into each step that passes; a move in the first half of a step counts for that step, a later one for the next.
  • Record arms live recording: while it's playing, touching the pad writes your live position into whichever step is currently passing, overdub-style — steps you don't touch keep whatever they already had. Hold the ratchet bar up at the same time to record ratchets, or use the bar on its own to re-cut steps that are already there.
  • Stepped / smooth (the key beside record): stepped gives each hit one position. Smooth lets a ringing hit follow your finger, and recording keeps the whole gesture through each step and its ratchets. It only affects new touches, so one sequence can mix both.
  • Choke (the cut-off wave) makes the kit monophonic: a new hit cuts off whatever's still ringing.
  • Drum engine layers: body (pitched sine with FM), noise, metal (808-style hat squares), resonator (a struck physical model that morphs from string to drum skin to bar), then a filter, a wavefolder and drive.
  • Undo / redo (or Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z) step back and forward through edits — steps, lanes, kit and engine tweaks, all of it.
  • The die in the top bar randomizes whichever page you're on: the sequence, the current lane, the whole kit or the drum you're editing. Drag it further right for a stronger roll.
  • Swipe left or right, or use the icons beside the title, to switch between the pad, lanes, whole kit, drum engine and this page.
